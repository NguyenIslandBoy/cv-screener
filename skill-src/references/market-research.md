# Job Market Research

When the user asks you to research the job market for a role, analyze by: role, location,
seniority level, industry, skill demand, competition level, salary range, entry barrier.

For each role, provide the following sections.

## Role Overview

Explain what the role usually does in real companies — not the idealized job-posting
description, but what the day-to-day actually involves.

## Required Skills

Separate into four categories:

### Must-Have Technical Skills
Essential technical skills for the role.

### Nice-to-Have Technical Skills
Useful but non-essential.

### Soft Skills
Communication, collaboration, analytical, and problem-solving skills needed.

### Domain Knowledge
Any industry or business knowledge that helps (e.g. fintech regulatory awareness, healthcare
data privacy norms).

## Common Tools and Technologies

Reference pool (not exhaustive — use what's relevant to the specific role): Python, SQL,
Excel, Power BI, Tableau, Pandas, NumPy, Scikit-learn, TensorFlow, PyTorch, Spark, Airflow,
Docker, Git, FastAPI, Django, PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB, dbt, Snowflake, Databricks, AWS,
Azure, GCP, LangChain, LlamaIndex, vector databases, REST APIs, CI/CD tools.

## Market Demand

Classify as one or more of: Growing, Competitive, Saturated, Niche, Declining, Strong for
entry-level candidates, Strong for experienced candidates only. Justify the classification —
don't just label it.

## Entry Barrier

Classify the role against the user's current profile:

- Easy fit
- Realistic fit
- Stretch fit
- Difficult fit
- Not recommended yet

Explain the reasoning. If you don't have the user's profile yet, classify generically and
note that the assessment will sharpen once you have their CV.

## Salary Expectations

Estimate ranges based on target location, role, and seniority. If precise data isn't
available, give a defensible range and flag the uncertainty rather than presenting a single
confident number — use web search for current data if location/role specifics matter and you
don't have reliable figures.

## Job Search Keywords

Suggest search terms for LinkedIn, Indeed, Glassdoor, Otta, Wellfound, Google Jobs, company
career pages, university job boards. Include both common titles and adjacent/alternate titles
companies use for the same role (e.g. "Analytics Engineer" vs "Data Engineer, Analytics" vs
"BI Engineer").

## Best Role Matches

When comparing multiple roles for the user, rank with this table:

| Rank | Role | Fit Level | Why It Fits | Main Gaps |
|---:|---|---|---|---|

Fit levels should vary honestly — don't rank every role as a strong fit if the evidence
doesn't support it.
