# Cover Letters & Outreach Messages

## Cover Letters

Make every cover letter:

- Specific to the company (not a template with the company name swapped in)
- Specific to the role
- Professional but natural-sounding
- Short enough to actually be read
- Focused on value the user brings, not just enthusiasm
- Connected to the user's real projects, skills, and background

### Structure

1. **Opening** — the role being applied for and why.
2. **Fit** — relevant experience, technical skills, or projects, tied directly to what the
   role needs.
3. **Company connection** — why this company or role specifically interests the user (requires
   genuine company-specific detail, not generic "I admire your mission" filler).
4. **Closing** — confident and polite call to action.

### Avoid

Generic filler like:
> I am passionate about technology.

### Use instead

Concrete, specific sentences like:
> My experience building machine learning pipelines, analyzing structured datasets, and
> communicating technical findings aligns closely with the role's focus on data-driven
> product improvement.

Default length: 250–400 words unless the user asks for a longer version.

## LinkedIn & Email Outreach Messages

For contacting recruiters, hiring managers, professors, founders, or employees:

- Polite, clear, specific
- Not desperate
- Easy to reply to
- Under 150 words where possible

### Structure for networking messages

1. Who the user is
2. Why they're reaching out
3. Relevant fit
4. A simple, low-effort request

### Example template

> Hi [Name], I'm currently exploring [role type] opportunities in [field]. I noticed your
> work at [company] focuses on [specific area], which aligns with my background in
> [skill/project]. I'd be grateful for any advice on applying or understanding what the team
> looks for in candidates. Thank you.

Customize the bracketed sections with real specifics from the user's background and the
recipient's actual work — a message that could be sent to anyone reads as low-effort and gets
ignored. If the user hasn't given enough detail about the recipient to personalize this,
ask rather than filling in something generic.
