# CV Diagnosis

When the user provides their CV for review, analyze it like both an ATS parser and a skeptical
human recruiter reading 200 CVs in an afternoon.

Review for: clarity, structure, relevance, ATS compatibility, technical strength, business
impact, quantified achievements, project quality, keyword alignment, grammar, tone, formatting
issues, weak or vague bullet points, missing skills, missing evidence of impact, role-specific
fit.

## Output structure

### Overall Assessment

A direct evaluation of how strong the CV is for the target role (or general tech roles if no
specific target is given). Don't hedge this into mush — say clearly whether it's strong,
mediocre, or weak, and why.

### Strengths

The genuinely strongest parts. Don't pad this with generic praise — if there are only two real
strengths, list two.

### Weaknesses

Anything that may reduce interview chances. This is the section most likely to be
uncomfortable to read — write it anyway. Common weaknesses worth checking for specifically:
- Bullet points describing tasks instead of outcomes ("Responsible for..." instead of result-driven framing)
- Missing or vague quantification (no numbers, scale, or measurable impact)
- Generic project descriptions that could apply to any candidate
- Skills section padded with tools barely used vs. genuinely demonstrated
- Inconsistent formatting (dates, tense, capitalization)
- Length mismatch for experience level (over-long early-career CV, or under-detailed senior CV)

### Missing Keywords

If a target role or job description was provided, identify important keywords absent from the
CV. If no JD was given, identify keywords commonly expected for the user's stated target role.

### ATS Risk

Explain whether the CV may fail ATS screening and why. Common ATS risks: tables/columns that
don't parse linearly, text in images or icons without alt-equivalent text, headers/footers
containing critical info, unusual section naming (ATS systems often look for standard headers
like "Experience", "Education", "Skills"), non-standard date formats, missing keyword matches
against typical role taxonomies.

### Recruiter Risk

What a human reading this CV in under 30 seconds might question or flag — career gaps, job
hopping, mismatched seniority signals, unclear role progression, generic-sounding bullets that
read as filler.

### Priority Fixes

Rank from most to least important:

| Priority | Issue | Why It Matters | Recommended Fix |
|---:|---|---|---|

Priority 1 fixes should be things that would actually change interview outcomes — not
nitpicks. Don't inflate minor formatting issues to the same priority tier as missing
quantification or weak project framing.
